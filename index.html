How the model works

Five rules in plain words, how a parent is priced, and the path every session takes from verification to payment.

The five rules

  1. Teacher pay comes from the teacher's agreement. The Manager drafts it, and the Admin sets the lowest and highest session rate that can be approved without an exception.
  2. The parent's price is set by the pricing arrangement. Usually the teacher's rate plus an operational fee the Admin configures.
  3. Manager pay is a percentage of the teacher's session rate. It is defined in the Manager's own agreement.
  4. Parents pay before (prepaid) or after (postpaid) the sessions. The Admin decides which arrangements the organization accepts.
  5. In the first release the platform does not move money. Payments happen outside it. The platform records them with evidence and the Admin verifies them.

Model A: rate plus fee

The parent pays the teacher's rate plus a percentage fee. At SAR 100 and 5%, the parent pays SAR 105. This is the model drawn in the charts.

Model B: fixed price

The parent pays a fixed amount the organization sets. Teacher and Manager pay are still calculated from their own agreements. No fee is added automatically.

Prepaid

The parent pays in advance. A session is allowed only against a verified payment, not just an uploaded receipt. Unused prepaid balance remains recorded on the Parent account. A refund requires an Admin-managed transfer and proof, followed by the Parent confirming receipt.

Postpaid

The Parent pays after sessions on an invoice with a due date. Only the Admin can approve postpaid, separately for each Parent. Overdue accounts are tracked, and the platform can hold upcoming sessions until the overdue issue is resolved.

How a session becomes money

Select a stage. The money side starts when a session is verified, not when it is scheduled.

Customer billing

Parents owe the organization
  • Invoices, prepaid balances, overdue aging
  • Reduced only by verified, allocated payments

Staff compensation

The organization owes teachers, Managers and eligible Admin staff
  • Earnings, approvals, payments, remaining payable
  • Not cancelled because a parent has not paid

SaaS subscription

The organization owes the platform operator
  • Own agreement, invoices and balance
  • Not deducted from a parent's payment

These three are never shown as one balance. Each has its own records, status changes, authorization rules and audit trail.

What the first release does not do

  • It does not process payments or transfer money.
  • It does not charge SaaS subscriptions.
  • It does not add interest, penalties or collection fees without an approved rule and legal review.
  • It does not treat an unpaid invoice as cash, or an operational fee as profit.
  • It does not cancel a teacher's earned pay because a parent has not paid.

System behaviour

How the platform applies the financial model

This section turns the proposed financial model into an end-to-end operating workflow: who takes each action, which record the platform creates, what changes financially, and what each role is allowed to see. The example percentages and amounts remain illustrative until approved.

Core rule

A scheduled session is not yet a financial transaction. The platform applies the relevant contract and pricing rules when the session outcome is verified. A calculated amount is not automatically earned, billed, collected, paid, or available cash; each state must be represented separately.

1. Configure the rules before using them

The Admin maintains effective-dated configuration rather than relying on hardcoded business outcomes. Configuration should include:

  • Minimum and maximum permitted teacher rates, contract approval permissions, and the process for authorized exceptions.
  • Available parent pricing models: teacher rate plus an operational fee, or a configured fixed customer price.
  • Operational-fee rates and scope; Manager compensation rules; and any eligible Admin/staff compensation agreements.
  • Approved prepaid and postpaid plans, eligibility, invoice due-date rules, payment verification requirements, and any authorization needed to override a restriction.
  • Session outcome policies, including attendance, cancellation, late cancellation, rescheduling, disputes, refunds and other exceptions. These remain configurable until the business owner approves the actual rules.
  • Operating-cost categories, required reserves, approval thresholds, and how funds may be allocated to development or expansion.

Every financial record stores the exact contract, pricing model, policy version, rate and configuration that applied at the time. Updating a setting affects future eligible records; it must not silently rewrite existing agreements, finalized charges, earnings, invoices or payments.

2. Agree and approve the teacher contract

  1. The Manager drafts an agreement for the teacher, including the session rate and applicable compensation terms.
  2. The platform validates the proposed rate against the Admin-configured minimum and maximum.
  3. An in-range agreement follows the configured approval workflow. An out-of-range rate is blocked unless an authorized exception path is explicitly enabled and approved.
  4. The accepted agreement becomes the source for future eligible session calculations. Amendments are versioned, dated and auditable; earlier sessions retain their original terms.

Result: an approved, versioned agreement—not an immediate payment. The teacher and authorized Manager can see the applicable terms; the Admin can inspect the complete record and its approval history.

3. Select a parent plan and establish payment eligibility

  1. The Parent chooses from plans that the Admin has approved and that are available for the family's arrangement.
  2. For a prepaid plan, the Parent submits payment externally and provides the requested reference or evidence. The platform records the report as unverified until the Admin checks the actual receipt.
  3. Only a verified and correctly allocated payment increases the verified prepaid balance. An uploaded receipt or Parent's claim alone does not make funds available.
  4. For a postpaid plan, the platform requires an explicit Admin approval tied to that individual Parent, then records the agreed terms, invoice cadence and due date. The Admin who authorizes postpaid accepts the collection risk for that Parent. Postpaid is never enabled globally by a single switch.
  5. Before a session is authorized, the platform checks the applicable plan and its configured eligibility rules. It must not invent a universal rule for low balance, absence or cancellation.

Result: the Parent sees the selected plan, verified prepaid balance where applicable, outstanding invoices, due dates and payment status. The Manager sees only the operational eligibility information needed to manage the arrangement; the Teacher does not see the Parent's unrelated financial details.

4. Conduct the session and verify its outcome

  1. The schedule records that the session was planned; it does not itself create a finalized charge or earning.
  2. The relevant participants submit the attendance/session outcome using the platform's defined workflow.
  3. The authorized role reviews exceptions or disputes where required. The outcome is finalized only when the configured verification conditions are met.
  4. The platform applies the approved policy for the recorded outcome. Attendance, student absence, teacher absence, late cancellation, cancellation, rescheduling and disputed sessions are distinct facts; the system must not hardcode one universal billing result for them.

Result: a verified session outcome linked to the student, teacher, contract, schedule and relevant policy version. A correction after finalization must create an auditable adjustment or reversal, not erase history.

5. Calculate separate financial records

After the session outcome qualifies under the approved policy, the system calculates the relevant records independently. For a rate-plus-fee example:

Teacher rate
SAR 100

Illustrative teacher compensation basis, subject to the agreement's actual terms.

Operational fee (5%)
SAR 5

Illustrative customer fee; not automatically profit or available cash.

Parent charge
SAR 105

Illustrative customer charge before applicable adjustments.

If the example Manager agreement defines compensation as 2% of the teacher rate, the illustrative Manager amount is SAR 2 and the residual operational allocation is SAR 3 before other costs, collection losses, refunds, taxes or other applicable items. These percentages are examples, not approved rates.

The platform creates linked but separate records for the customer charge or prepaid consumption, teacher compensation, Manager compensation where applicable, and operational allocation. The exact point at which compensation becomes earned or payable must follow the approved agreement and policy; the system must not assume that a session completion means staff have already been paid.

6. Apply the parent's billing arrangement

Prepaid

  1. Start from verified, allocated prepaid funds.
  2. When a qualifying session is finalized, record the charge against the appropriate balance according to the approved policy.
  3. Show the Parent the opening balance, verified top-ups, session charges, adjustments and resulting balance.
  4. Keep any disputed charge, refund or unused-balance treatment visible and governed by an explicit policy.

Postpaid

  1. Accumulate eligible finalized charges according to the approved billing period.
  2. Issue an invoice or statement with its items, total, issue date and due date.
  3. Record reported payments separately from payments verified and allocated to the invoice.
  4. Calculate outstanding and overdue amounts from the invoice and verified allocations; retain collection actions and adjustments in the audit history.

A payment report, a verified receipt, an allocated payment and a settled invoice are different states. A single payment may be allocated across permitted items, and a partial payment must leave the correct residual outstanding balance.

7. Record and verify external staff payments

  1. The platform calculates staff amounts from the approved agreements and qualifying financial records.
  2. The system shows the amount's status—calculated, earned, payable or otherwise due—according to the approved contractual rules.
  3. An authorized Admin or finance role records an external disbursement with recipient, amount, currency, date, method/reference, related payable items and supporting evidence.
  4. The disbursement remains reported/unverified until the authorized reviewer verifies that the transfer actually occurred.
  5. Only the verified amount is reflected as paid. Partial payments leave the unpaid remainder visible. Corrections and reversals retain their history.

Recording a payment is not the same as initiating a transfer. The initial release records and verifies external payments; it does not itself move money through a payment gateway or bank integration.

8. Role-specific financial views

Parent

  • Chosen payment plan and its terms.
  • Verified prepaid balance or postpaid invoices, due dates and outstanding amount.
  • Session-level charges, credits, adjustments and payment allocations.
  • Payment submission status and what evidence or action is still needed.

A Parent can access only their own account and linked children within their authorized scope.

Teacher

  • Own approved contract and applicable rate/terms.
  • Assigned sessions and their finalized outcomes.
  • Session-level compensation, earning/payable status, adjustments and verified payments.
  • Relevant payment references or statements without exposing other staff's financial information.

Manager

  • Managed teachers' contract status and rate validation/exception workflow.
  • Assigned students' operational billing eligibility and session status.
  • Own compensation terms, calculated earnings, payable history and verified payments.
  • Operational summaries, exceptions and overdue follow-up within the Manager's authorized scope.

Manager visibility into individual Parent financial details must be limited to what the role needs to perform its duties.

Admin

  • All approved pricing, contracts, plans, policy versions and controlled exceptions.
  • Customer charges, prepaid liabilities/balances, invoices, receivables, verified collections and overdue aging.
  • Teacher, Manager and eligible Admin/staff compensation, payables, disbursements and verification state.
  • Operating costs, reserves, actual cash, forecast, allocations, adjustments and complete audit history.

9. Admin's organization-level cash and allocation workflow

Per-session allocations are not the same as the organization's cash position. The Admin financial workspace should guide decisions in this order:

  1. Reconcile actual cash. Start from verified opening balances and actual bank/cash records; account for verified receipts, disbursements, transfers and adjustments. Do not count uncollected invoices as cash.
  2. Review incoming collections. Separate verified received money from reported payments, overdue receivables and forecasts. Show aging, expected collection timing and collection-risk scenarios.
  3. Identify commitments. Show staff payables, supplier obligations, taxes or other approved liabilities, refunds/credits and their due dates. Whether staff compensation is due before customer collection must follow the contract and owner-approved policy.
  4. Cover operating costs and required reserves. Review actual and forecast fixed/variable costs, essential operating expenses and the target reserve. Record each cost with category, date, amount, payee, evidence, approval and payment status.
  5. Calculate genuinely allocatable cash. Derive it from reconciled cash after considering approved commitments, required reserves and planned costs. Label forecasts and assumptions; do not present an estimate as a bank balance.
  6. Review discretionary allocation. Only after the above review should Admin consider development, expansion, equipment, contingency or other approved uses. Proposed allocations require the applicable approval and must be recorded with amount, purpose, date, decision-maker and rationale.
  7. Reconcile after action. Once an expense or allocation is actually paid, record and verify it, update cash and forecast figures, and retain the before/after audit trail.

Important: the operational allocation is a management calculation, not proof of profit. Profitability requires complete and appropriately classified revenue and costs. Available cash is a liquidity measure; receivables, payables, reserves and forecasts must remain distinct.

11. System safeguards and acceptance criteria

  • Authorization: enforce role and relationship scope on the server for every read and write; hiding a screen element is not sufficient.
  • Idempotency: retrying session finalization or billing must not create duplicate charges, earnings or allocations.
  • Controlled transitions: validate every state change and require explicit authority for verification, overrides, refunds, write-offs and adjustments.
  • Immutable history: finalized financial records are corrected with linked reversals/adjustments and reasons, not silently overwritten or deleted.
  • Consistent calculation: use the same authoritative server-side calculation and stored rule version across dashboards, statements, invoices and reports; use decimal-safe money handling and explicit currency.
  • Separation: customer billing, prepaid funds, staff compensation, operating allocations, expenses/cash and SaaS subscription billing remain separate financial domains linked by references rather than collapsed into one balance.
  • Verification: reported payments do not change verified balances or paid status until the authorized verification and allocation steps succeed.
  • Traceability: every charge, earning, receipt, payment, cost, adjustment and discretionary allocation can be traced to its source record, policy, actor and evidence.
  • Reconciliation: totals reconcile across session ledgers, customer statements, staff statements and cash records, while unpaid receivables and payables remain visible.
  • Testing: cover prepaid and postpaid flows, partial payments, duplicate retries, rejected/out-of-range contracts, authorized exceptions, session corrections, refunds/credits, failed verification, overdue invoices, partial staff payments, permission boundaries and cash/ledger reconciliation.

12. Decisions that remain open

The system should be designed to support these policies without inventing them. The responsible owners and legal/accounting advisers must decide and document them before the corresponding rules are enabled:

  • The timing and schedule of staff compensation still follow the applicable agreements, but staff compensation is not payable before the corresponding customer payment has been collected and verified.
  • The exact legal drafting and classification of the marketplace-style relationship should be reflected in the applicable agreements; the intended commercial model is described in the confirmed operating decisions.
  • The configured tax rate and invoice calculation are business settings. The actual invoice format and issuance process must be implemented to meet applicable e-invoicing and tax requirements.
  • Detailed absence, cancellation, rescheduling and session-correction policies are outside the current scope unless separately specified. The confirmed unused-balance refund workflow is documented in the operating decisions.
  • Only the Admin may approve postpaid, individually per Parent. Additional credit-limit, write-off and refund policies are not being expanded in this release beyond the confirmed refund workflow and upcoming-session hold.
  • Exact compensation schedule details and internal approval thresholds remain governed by the relevant agreements and Admin configuration; staff payments cannot precede verified customer collection.
  • Payment processing and automatic bank integration are out of scope for the initial release. The platform records uploaded evidence and manual Admin verification; invoice generation is included in the described billing workflow.
Owner decisions · Updated 10 October 2026

Confirmed operating decisions

These are the owner's current decisions for how the platform must behave. They replace the earlier “unresolved” labels for these topics. This section describes the initial product scope, not legal advice.

1. Postpaid and collection risk

  • Admin approval is per Parent. Only the Admin can authorize postpaid, and each approval applies to a named Parent. There is no global postpaid enablement that automatically grants every Parent credit.
  • Risk follows the approval. The Admin who permits postpaid for that Parent accepts the collection risk if the Parent never pays. The system must retain who approved it, when, and the terms that were accepted.
  • No staff payment before collection. Teacher and Manager compensation is not payable before the related customer payment has actually been collected and verified. Calculated or earned amounts must not be displayed as already paid.
  • Overdue enforcement is deliberately limited. The platform tracks overdue balances and may hold the Parent's upcoming session from starting. Legal proceedings, debt recovery and other legal consequences are outside the current scope.

2. Unused prepaid balance and refunds

  1. Unused prepaid funds remain visible as a balance on the Parent's account; they do not disappear at the end of a billing period.
  2. The Parent submits a refund request. The platform records the amount, reason, status and request history for Admin review.
  3. The Admin executes the refund transfer outside the platform and uploads the transfer proof, including the relevant date, amount and reference.
  4. The Parent must separately confirm that the money was received. Uploading transfer proof alone does not close the request.
  5. The request closes only after the Admin's transfer evidence is recorded and the Parent's receipt confirmation is recorded. The platform itself does not move money.

3. Marketplace-style commercial relationship

  • The organization operates as a service hub/marketplace: Teachers offer services through the organization's platform in return for an agreed percentage or fee from the income, under the applicable agreement.
  • A Manager manages the Teachers assigned to them and receives a configured/agreed percentage of the relevant income, as set out in the Manager's agreement.
  • The exact contractual wording and legal classification should be represented in the organization's agreements. The product must calculate from the approved agreement, not infer a relationship from a role name.

4. Payment verification and invoice issuance

  1. The customer uploads payment receipt/evidence and supplies any required transfer reference.
  2. The Admin manually verifies the claim against the organization's bank account. An uploaded receipt alone is not proof that funds arrived.
  3. After the Admin verifies the funds, the payment is recorded as received/allocated and the platform issues its e-invoice using the configured invoice and tax settings.
  4. The system keeps the uploaded evidence, verification decision, verifier, timestamp, bank/reference details and invoice link in the audit trail. Corrections must be traceable rather than silently overwriting history.

5. Tax configuration and parent bill

  • The Admin configures the applicable tax rate/policy in the system.
  • The Parent's bill calculation shows the applicable tax as a separate, understandable component of the bill total, rather than hiding it inside a staff rate or operational allocation.
  • The configuration must be applied consistently to the relevant bill/invoice. The implementation must support the applicable e-invoice format and tax requirements; a configurable rate is not a substitute for required compliance.

6. Hosting and tenant isolation

  • The provider hosts the service. Hosting is part of the SaaS service relationship; the tutoring organization does not self-host its tenant as the normal service model.
  • Each tenant receives an independent server cluster. Application services, databases, files, credentials, secrets, backups and operational access must be isolated per tenant rather than shared in one tenant's runtime.
  • SaaS subscription billing remains separate from tutoring customer billing and staff compensation. Subscription pricing is set in the provider–tenant commercial agreement.

7. Scope boundary

For this release, do not expand into legal debt collection, legal proceedings, automated payment transfers, payment-gateway integration, or unrelated financial/accounting features. The platform records evidence, applies the confirmed rules, performs the calculations, issues the configured invoice, and maintains the audit trail.

01Executive summary

The tutoring platform will coordinate the financial relationship between parents, teachers, Managers, the tutoring organization, and the SaaS platform operator.

The financial model is based on five principles:

  1. The teacher's compensation is determined by the agreement with the student/customer arrangement, including the agreed session rate and applicable compensation terms.
  2. The parent-facing price is determined by the applicable pricing arrangement, including a configurable operational fee.
  3. The Manager's compensation is a configurable percentage of the teacher's agreed session rate, as defined by the Manager's compensation agreement.
  4. Parents may use approved prepaid or postpaid arrangements. The Admin controls which payment arrangements the organization accepts.
  5. Customer payments and staff payments are made externally in the initial release and recorded in the platform with supporting evidence and Admin verification.

The platform will calculate financial obligations, generate customer bills and statements, record payment evidence, track overdue balances, and report staff compensation and operating allocations.

It will not initially process payments or automatically transfer funds.

Three financial domains must remain separate:

  • Customer billing: Amounts parents owe the tutoring organization.
  • Staff compensation: Amounts the organization owes teachers, Managers, and eligible Admin employees.
  • SaaS subscription billing: Amounts the tutoring organization owes the platform operator.

The platform will also distinguish between amounts calculated, amounts earned, amounts billed, payments reported, payments verified, and cash actually available.

03Teacher contracts and permitted session rates

3.1 Teacher agreement

The teacher enters into an agreement through the Manager or another authorized representative.

The agreement establishes the applicable session rate and relevant conditions, including:

  • Agreed rate and currency
  • Applicable student/customer arrangement
  • Effective date and contract status
  • Session and attendance requirements
  • Compensation eligibility
  • Prepaid or postpaid customer arrangement, where relevant
  • Payment schedule and due dates
  • Contract renewal and termination
  • Treatment of outstanding earnings when the agreement ends

The system must preserve the terms and rate that applied to each financial record.

3.2 Admin-configured rate boundaries

The Admin configures the minimum and maximum permitted teacher session rates.

Illustrative configuration:

Setting Example
Minimum permitted rate SAR 80
Maximum permitted rate SAR 150
Operational-fee percentage 5%

These are examples, not approved commercial rates.

When a Manager drafts a contract, the platform validates the proposed session rate.

  • Below the minimum: block the normal approval path or require an authorized exception.
  • Within the range: permit the contract to proceed through the normal approval process.
  • Above the maximum: block the normal approval path or require an authorized exception.

The Admin must control exception permissions and the system must audit any approved exception.

New limits must not silently alter existing contracts. Changes to an existing rate must follow the contract's amendment and approval requirements.

04Customer pricing and the operational fee

The platform supports two parent-pricing models.

Model A — Teacher session rate plus operational fee

The parent-facing bill includes the teacher's session rate plus a configurable operational fee.

Let:

  • TT = agreed teacher session rate
  • ror_o = operational-fee percentage
  • OO = operational fee
  • PP = parent total before any separately applicable taxes or adjustments

Then:

O=T×ro O=T\times r_o

P=T+O P=T+O

Illustrative example:

Item Calculation Amount
Teacher session rate Contracted rate SAR 100
Operational fee SAR 100 × 5% SAR 5
Parent total SAR 100 + SAR 5 SAR 105

The Admin controls the operational-fee percentage and its applicable scope and effective dates.

The operational fee is a configurable policy, not a hardcoded 5% rate.

Model B — Fixed customer price

The organization configures a fixed customer-facing price for the applicable arrangement.

The parent is charged the configured amount. The system calculates teacher and Manager compensation independently under their agreements.

The platform must not automatically add an operational fee to a fixed customer price unless the applicable arrangement explicitly defines that fee as an additional component.

Arrangement selection

The organization may use different pricing models for different arrangements, subject to approved scope and precedence rules.

The platform must preserve the pricing model and applicable configuration used for each issued bill.

05Operational-fee allocation and Manager compensation

The Manager's compensation is based on a configurable percentage of the teacher's agreed session rate.

The percentage is defined by the applicable Manager compensation agreement. It is not inherently 40% of the operational-fee pool, although the amounts can coincide under particular rates.

Illustrative example:

  • Teacher session rate: SAR 100
  • Operational fee: 5% of teacher rate = SAR 5
  • Manager compensation rate: 2% of teacher rate = SAR 2
Financial component Amount
Teacher's contractual earning SAR 100
Manager's compensation allocation SAR 2
Organization's residual operational allocation SAR 3
Parent's total charge SAR 105

Under this example, the Manager receives 40% of the SAR 5 operational-fee pool, and SAR 3 remains allocated to organizational operations.

This outcome is a consequence of the chosen percentages, not the definition of the Manager's compensation method.

Configurable rates

The Admin configures the operational-fee percentage based on the organization's financial forecast, including expected fixed costs and dynamic income.

The Manager compensation percentage is configured according to the applicable Manager agreement and organizational approval rules.

The system must validate the combined configuration. For example, it must not silently produce a negative residual operating allocation if the Manager's compensation exceeds the operational fee.

If the configured rates are commercially inconsistent, the system should flag the arrangement for correction or authorized approval.

Operational allocation is not profit

The residual allocation is intended to support organizational operations such as hosting, technical support, maintenance, administration, and other approved expenses.

It is not automatically net profit.

The Admin dashboard should distinguish the operational allocation from actual recorded expenses and from any properly calculated profit measure.

06Prepaid and postpaid customer arrangements

The platform must support two explicitly identified payment-plan types.

These plans control the timing and conditions of customer payment. They do not, by themselves, change the agreed teacher session rate or the Manager's compensation calculation.

6.1 Prepaid plan

Under a prepaid arrangement, the parent must satisfy the applicable advance-payment requirement before the relevant service is authorized.

The system must identify:

  • The prepaid arrangement
  • The amount required in advance
  • The applicable covered sessions or service period
  • The payment deadline
  • Whether the advance payment has been recorded and verified
  • The remaining prepaid balance or unused credit
  • Which sessions or services have consumed that balance

The Admin decides which prepaid arrangements the organization accepts and configures their permitted terms.

A session should not be treated as authorized under a prepaid requirement merely because a payment receipt has been uploaded. The applicable payment must have been verified and correctly allocated, subject to the approved policy.

The exact consequences of insufficient prepaid balance, cancellation, unused credit, and refunds require policy approval.

6.2 Postpaid plan

Under a postpaid arrangement, the parent pays after the applicable service has been delivered or after the agreed billing period.

The platform must identify the arrangement as postpaid and apply its approved billing schedule.

It must track:

  • Qualifying sessions
  • Charges generated
  • Billing period
  • Invoice issue date
  • Due date
  • Payments recorded and verified
  • Outstanding and overdue balances

The system must not silently treat a postpaid customer as prepaid or vice versa.

The organization must define which customers are eligible for postpaid arrangements and who may approve them.

6.3 Payment-plan enforcement

The platform must validate the payment plan before allowing a session or billing action that depends on it.

However, the precise enforcement rules—for example, whether a prepaid balance shortfall blocks booking, joining, or future scheduling—must follow the approved operational policy.

07Session verification and financial record creation

The financial lifecycle begins with a verified qualifying event, not merely a scheduled session.

Stage 1 — Contract approval

The teacher agreement, customer pricing arrangement, operational-fee configuration, and relevant compensation agreements are approved and active.

Stage 2 — Session outcome

The session is conducted, cancelled, or otherwise resolved through the existing session workflow.

Stage 3 — Verification

The applicable authorized workflow confirms the session outcome and relevant evidence.

Stage 4 — Financial calculation

The system resolves the applicable contract, pricing model, operational fee, compensation agreements, and billing policies.

It then creates separate linked financial records for:

  • Customer charge or credit
  • Teacher compensation earning, when earned under the agreement
  • Manager compensation earning, when earned under the agreement
  • Operational allocation
  • Applicable calculation snapshots and source references

Stage 5 — Customer billing

For postpaid arrangements, the applicable charge enters the billing workflow and is invoiced according to the approved schedule.

For prepaid arrangements, the system applies the approved prepaid-balance rules and records the consumption or allocation of the advance payment.

Stage 6 — Payment recording and verification

The parent or other payer pays externally. An authorized user uploads the supporting receipt or evidence and enters the amount.

The Admin checks whether the money was actually received and whether the amount is correct. Once verified, the payment is allocated to the relevant customer balance.

Stage 7 — Staff payment recording

Teacher and Manager compensation payments are made externally and recorded against the appropriate payable records.

The system updates payment and outstanding balances without rewriting the original earnings.

08Payment verification procedure

The initial platform release does not process money. It records external payments and supports a controlled verification workflow.

Required payment evidence

A payment record should include:

  • Payer or recipient
  • Amount and currency
  • Payment date
  • Payment method or external channel, where applicable
  • Receipt or supporting evidence upload
  • External transaction reference, if available
  • Relevant invoice or compensation statement
  • User who recorded the payment
  • Verification status
  • Admin verifier and verification timestamp
  • Reason for rejection or correction, where applicable

Verification states

The proposed workflow is:

  1. Awaiting verification: Evidence has been uploaded and the amount entered.
  2. Verified: The Admin has confirmed receipt or disbursement, as appropriate, and checked the amount and allocation.
  3. Rejected: The evidence or amount is invalid or does not match the actual transaction.
  4. Corrected or reversed: An existing entry requires a traceable correction.

The uploaded receipt is supporting evidence; it is not proof by itself that the funds reached the intended account.

A payment must not reduce an outstanding balance until it has been verified and correctly allocated.

The system must preserve verification history and must not silently overwrite a previously verified transaction.

For staff payments, the same principle applies: the system records the evidence and the Admin verifies that the external payment was actually made. Whether a second person must review certain payments remains a governance decision for the owners.

09Customer receivables and overdue balances

The platform must distinguish an issued invoice from a payment and from the remaining amount owed.

Outstanding balance

At a basic level:

Outstanding Balance=Issued Charges−Verified Allocated Payments−Applicable Credits+Applicable Adjustments \text{Outstanding Balance} = \text{Issued Charges} - \text{Verified Allocated Payments} - \text{Applicable Credits} + \text{Applicable Adjustments}

The final calculation must account for reversals, refunds, and credit balances according to approved accounting rules.

Overdue definition

An invoice becomes overdue when its approved due date has passed and it still has an outstanding balance, subject to the applicable dispute and credit policies.

Proposed reporting categories:

Category Meaning
Not yet due Payment deadline has not passed
1–7 days overdue Short-term overdue
8–30 days overdue Follow-up required
31–60 days overdue Elevated collection concern
61–90 days overdue Serious collection concern
More than 90 days overdue Escalated review

These categories are configurable reporting suggestions, not legal definitions.

Overdue workflow

  1. An invoice is issued under the applicable payment plan.
  2. The due date is recorded.
  3. The system tracks verified allocated payments.
  4. If a balance remains after the due date, the invoice is flagged as overdue.
  5. The Manager and Admin see the balance and aging category within their authorized scope.
  6. The organization follows its approved reminder and collection procedure.
  7. Payment, credit, dispute resolution, or an authorized adjustment updates the balance.

The platform must not automatically impose interest, penalties, or collection fees without an approved rule and appropriate legal review.

10Staff compensation and outstanding payables

The platform must maintain separate compensation records for teachers, Managers, and eligible Admin employees.

For each person, the system should report:

  • Compensation earned
  • Compensation pending verification or approval
  • Amount approved for payment
  • Amount paid and verified
  • Remaining payable balance
  • Contractual payment schedule
  • Overdue payable amount, where applicable
  • Related sessions, agreements, and adjustments

Teacher earnings

The teacher's earnings are governed by the applicable agreement. The financial engine must preserve the agreed rate and terms.

The customer payment plan determines when the parent is expected to pay. It does not automatically change the teacher's compensation agreement.

Important unresolved matter: Whether the organization carries the collection risk when a parent never pays remains outside the current agreed scope and must be decided at the owner/legal meeting.

Until that decision is made, the system must track customer receivables and staff payables independently. It must not assume that a teacher's earned compensation is automatically cancelled because a parent has not paid.

Manager earnings

Manager compensation is calculated from the agreed percentage of the teacher's session rate, subject to the applicable qualifying-session rules and contract.

The system must preserve the agreement version used in each calculation.

The compensation must not be calculated as a percentage of the operational-fee pool unless a specific agreement explicitly selects that alternative basis.

Admin employee compensation

Eligible Admin employees may have their own compensation agreements. Administrative permissions must not determine how an employee is compensated.

Owner distributions, dividends, and profit withdrawals remain distinct from employee compensation and are not included in the initial model unless separately approved.

Cash-flow forecasting

The Admin dashboard should forecast:

Expected inflows

  • Customer payments due
  • Estimated collections of overdue balances
  • Other approved revenue

Expected outflows

  • Teacher compensation payments due
  • Manager compensation payments due
  • Admin employee compensation due
  • Hosting and infrastructure
  • Technical support and maintenance
  • Administration and approved tools
  • Expected refunds and other approved obligations
  • SaaS subscription costs paid by the tutoring organization

Forecasts must be clearly labeled as estimates. An unpaid invoice is not guaranteed cash.

Operating reserve

The organization should establish a minimum cash reserve based on essential fixed expenses and near-term obligations.

The target should be configurable and approved by the owners with accounting advice. The dashboard should compare the target with actual reconciled cash.

A cash shortage warning must prompt planning and escalation; it must not silently cancel a contractual obligation or alter earned compensation.

12Operational allocation and break-even analysis

The Admin sets the operational-fee rate using the organization's forecast of fixed costs, variable costs, and expected income.

For the illustrative rates:

Item Amount
Teacher session rate SAR 100
Operational fee at 5% SAR 5
Manager commission at 2% of teacher rate SAR 2
Residual operating allocation SAR 3

If the relevant operating expense target is SAR 3,000 per month, and each qualifying session generates SAR 3 of collected residual allocation, the simple break-even volume for that expense pool is:

SAR 3,000SAR 3=1,000 sessions \frac{\text{SAR }3{,}000}{\text{SAR }3} = 1{,}000\text{ sessions}

This estimate assumes the relevant fee is fully collected and excludes other costs, refunds, taxes, and unmodeled revenue.

The organization should evaluate:

  • Expected session volume
  • Teacher-rate distribution
  • Operational-fee rate
  • Manager compensation commitments
  • Collection delays and defaults
  • Fixed hosting and support costs
  • Variable costs
  • Refunds and adjustments
  • Reserve requirements

The Admin dashboard should display operational fees billed and collected, Manager compensation, residual operating allocation, actual recorded expenses, and any supported operating contribution measure separately.

  • Teacher absence

  • Session dispute
  • Partial attendance
  • Refund
  • Incorrect payment record
  • Contract termination
  • Discount or special pricing arrangement

The system must preserve the original financial record and record corrections through traceable adjustments or refund records.

Discounts must identify which component they affect. They must not automatically reduce the teacher's contractual compensation without an applicable agreement and approved policy.

The handling of unused prepaid balances, refunds, disputed payments, and customer nonpayment remains subject to the decisions and legal review required for the final policy.

15SaaS subscription billing

SaaS subscription billing is a separate relationship between the tutoring organization and the platform operator.

It has its own commercial agreement, pricing plan, billing period, invoices, payments, and outstanding balances.

The platform operator's subscription revenue must not be confused with the tutoring organization's operational allocation. Nor should the subscription automatically be deducted from a parent's tutoring payment.

The service is hosted by the platform provider, so the tutoring organization is a hosted customer with a separate SaaS subscription arrangement. Subscription payment collection and automated transfers remain outside the initial release unless separately added.

SaaS pricing is a separate commercial agreement between the provider and each tenant. Each tenant is deployed in an independent server cluster; tenant data and services must not be shared across those clusters.

19Proposed operating model

The agreed direction is that the organization controls the financial rules, the Manager drafts teacher contracts within approved limits, the teacher earns compensation according to the applicable agreement, and the parent pays under an approved prepaid or postpaid plan.

The Admin configures the operational-fee percentage based on the organization's financial forecast. The Manager's compensation percentage is calculated against the teacher's agreed session rate, according to the Manager's agreement.

When a qualifying session is verified, the platform generates separate customer billing and staff compensation records. Customer payments and staff payments are made externally, recorded with supporting evidence, and verified by the Admin before being reflected as verified transactions.

The platform separately tracks customer receivables, staff payables, operational allocations, actual recorded expenses, and available cash. Overdue amounts are visible and traceable. Finalized records are corrected through auditable adjustments.

SaaS subscription billing remains a separate commercial and financial domain.

Conclusion

This model provides a structured financial operating framework without assuming that every outstanding invoice is cash, that every earned amount has been paid, or that the operational fee represents profit.

The operating direction is now specified for the current scope: per-Parent postpaid approval and risk ownership, no staff compensation before verified customer collection, a two-party refund closure workflow, overdue-session holds, configured tax in the bill calculation, manual receipt verification before issuing the platform invoice, a hosted SaaS relationship, and independent server clusters per tenant. The implementation should encode these rules while ensuring that required invoices and tax settings are configured in line with applicable requirements.

The owner decisions above define the current intended behaviour. Use the decision register for the remaining governance checks; do not treat those checks as permission to invent additional business policies.
